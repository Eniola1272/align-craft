## Selected explorations

**The decisions behind the design.**

Explore the user problem, the interface and the reasoning behind each concept. These are original studio studies, not commissioned client projects or measured results.

### Forma

A workspace concept that gives small teams a shared view of projects and progress, with the next action close at hand.

**Scope:** Product strategy · UI/UX design · Design system

**Design challenge:** Project updates spread across separate tools make it hard to see what needs attention. This concept brings the essentials into one workspace.

**Approach:** A compact navigation system leads into a project overview. Consistent status labels and progress indicators help people move between the big picture and individual tasks.

**Intended outcome:** Help a small team understand where work stands and decide what to do next.

### Gather

A homeware storefront concept that gives products room to tell their story while keeping the collection easy to browse.

**Scope:** Creative direction · E-commerce UX · Website design

**Design challenge:** A small collection needs a distinctive presentation and a straightforward shopping experience. The product story and the route to purchase have to work together.

**Approach:** Generous product framing sets the tone. Restrained navigation and a simple collection structure keep attention on the objects and the next step into the catalogue.

**Intended outcome:** Help customers understand the collection and find a product that interests them.

### Mono

A mobile wallet concept that puts balances, payments and recent activity within easy reach, so everyday money tasks are easier to follow.

**Scope:** Mobile UX · Interface design · Interactive prototype

**Design challenge:** A wallet needs to make the important information clear without crowding a small screen. People should be able to distinguish account status from actions they can take.

**Approach:** The balance has a clear place in the hierarchy. Direct payment actions and readable activity rows give the core journeys a consistent structure. All account data shown is illustrative.

**Intended outcome:** Make it easier to understand the account and choose the next payment action.

### Fieldwork

An operations concept that puts schedules and team locations in the same workspace, giving field and office teams a shared view of the day.

**Scope:** Workflow mapping · Dashboard UX · Interface design

**Design challenge:** Coordinating jobs becomes difficult when the schedule and field updates live in different places. Teams need a common view of what is happening.

**Approach:** The schedule sits beside a map, with explicit status labels and a clear hierarchy for time-sensitive jobs. The locations and schedules are sample data.

**Intended outcome:** Help teams connect a job’s status with its place in the day’s schedule.

### Petal

A plant-shop concept that pairs approachable product names with care cues, helping first-time owners find a place to start.

**Scope:** Art direction · Shopping experience · Responsive design

**Design challenge:** A first-time plant owner may not know what to choose. The shopping experience needs to introduce the collection without assuming specialist knowledge.

**Approach:** Plain-language product names and an inviting collection layout make the range approachable. Care cues give people useful context as they browse.

**Intended outcome:** Help someone identify a plant they want to learn more about and understand the care involved.

### Formly

A form-builder concept that keeps the question outline and respondent’s view together, making editing decisions easier to judge.

**Scope:** Product UX · Builder interface · Design system

**Design challenge:** Form builders can separate the editing controls from the experience people will receive. This concept keeps both in view.

**Approach:** A question outline provides structure, the editing canvas shows the result, and a clear publishing action marks the next step. Each area has a distinct job.

**Intended outcome:** Help people understand how their questions fit together before publishing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Before you commit.**

Ask us about the part you’re still weighing up.

### Is Align Craft the right fit for our project?

We work with founders and businesses who want design and engineering considered together. You may need a new product, a better experience for an existing one, or a website that represents the business properly. We start by agreeing what the work needs to achieve.

### Do we need a finished brief before we speak?

No. Bring what you know about the business, the users and the problem. Discovery helps us work through the unknowns and define a useful first phase. A finished specification is not a prerequisite for that conversation.

### Can you work on an existing website or product?

Yes. We can review the current experience and technical setup, then agree which improvements matter most. Share the product and tell us where customers or your team are running into difficulty.

### How do you scope and price the work?

We understand the requirements before quoting a defined phase. Your proposal sets out the deliverables, fee, milestones and acceptance criteria. If the scope changes, we discuss the effect on price and timing before proceeding.

### How much of our time will you need?

We agree review points and the decisions that need your input at the start. You’ll see progress throughout the phase, with specific feedback requests so your team can make decisions and keep the work moving.

### What will we receive at handover?

Your proposal defines the source files, account access and documentation included in the handover, along with ownership terms and any warranty. Ongoing maintenance and future improvements can be scoped separately.
